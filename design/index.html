Rethinking course registration for UCalgary students

Rain ERP, startup project through UCalgary Experience Ventures, fall 2026

The problem
Registering for courses means juggling degree requirements, personal time constraints, prerequisites, full sections, and timetable clashes, often across separate screens, with mistakes only surfacing at the last step.
What I designed
A guided five-step flow: review degree requirements, mark times you can't attend or would rather avoid, build a candidate schedule, review, then confirm. Planning never touches an existing enrollment until the student explicitly confirms a change.
Design choices
Problems are caught where they happen. Missing prerequisites are flagged on the requirements page, and the schedule builder names exactly which sections clash with each other or with the student's own unavailable times, then blocks review until they're resolved. Planned courses are always labelled "candidate, not enrolled" so nobody mistakes a draft for a registration.

User-centred design · Interactive prototype · Error prevention

Hive: running a shared home without the group chat chaos

SENG 513 web development project

The problem
Roommates juggle shared costs, chores, and house logistics across scattered group chats, and the friction turns into "we need to talk."
What I did
Designed and built a platform around the three things a shared home needs: splitting expenses and settling up, a fair chore rotation, and one place for house announcements, reminders, and grocery runs, kept separate from personal chats. It ships as a web app and a mobile app.
Design choices
A warm, honey-toned identity and a playful bee mascot keep a potentially tense subject, money and chores between friends, feeling friendly rather than administrative.

AuditEdge: making a dense professional workflow navigable

Independent project, in progress

The problem
An audit runs through many stages: engagement setup, trial balance mapping, risk assessment, fieldwork, and sign-off. Each has its own detail, and it's easy to lose your place.
What I'm doing
Designing the flow so auditors always know where an engagement stands and what comes next, drawing on my experience as a junior IT consultant at an audit firm. The home screen lays the whole file out as a sequence (engagement setup, then planning, then fieldwork) so the structure of the work is visible before you start.
Design choices
Auditors spend long sessions in their files, so the app offers three display modes: Day, Night, and a cooler Focus theme. Each mode keeps the same layout and controls, so switching never changes where anything is.
